# Writing guide

The reader is a recruiter skimming for about 10 seconds, and often an applicant
tracking system (ATS) before that. Write for both: the most relevant facts first,
plain standard headings, and concrete language.

## The TL;DR summary (always, at the top)

One or two short paragraphs, about 40-100 words in total, directly under the header.
It answers: who is this, what do they do best, and what are they looking for.

- **Paragraph 1:** role identity, domain and core strengths, drawn from the actual
  experience. For a tailored CV, echo the posting's role name and top requirements
  where they're true of the user.
- **Paragraph 2 (optional):** a standout achievement or what they're looking for next.
- No first person ("I", "my"), no clichés (results-driven, passionate, team player,
  proven track record), and no numbers that aren't in the sources. Leave out the
  years-of-experience claim unless the user confirmed the figure.

Example (every fact here is in the fictional sources):

> Data engineering team lead who builds and runs cloud data platforms for logistics
> and retail: lakehouse architecture on Databricks, batch and streaming pipelines,
> and dbt-based analytics engineering, with 10 years in data.
>
> Currently leads a team of four at Harborlight Logistics, where the new lakehouse cut
> monthly warehouse cost by about 35%. Looking for a senior data engineering or data
> platform lead role.

## Bullets

- Start with a strong past-tense verb for past roles and present tense for the current
  role (Built, Led, Designed, Migrated, Cut, Introduced, Owns).
- Pattern: **action + what + how/with what + result**. Include the result only if it's
  known. A bullet without a number is fine. A bullet with an invented number is not.
- 1-2 lines each. 3-6 bullets for the current role, 2-4 for recent past roles,
  0-2 for old ones.
- Merge overlapping bullets. Drop duties that any holder of the title would have
  ("attended meetings").
- Keep punctuation consistent: end every bullet with a period or none of them (the
  default is none).
- Don't bury the lead: in a tailored CV, the bullet most relevant to the posting goes
  first within each role.

## Tailoring (a posting was given)

You have a free hand, up to an entirely new version:

- rewrite the headline to match the target role when it truthfully describes the
  person. The headline is a positioning line, not a job title claim
- reorder sections (e.g. Skills above Experience for a skills-heavy posting)
- rewrite every bullet, promote relevant work, cut irrelevant work
- use the posting's vocabulary for things the user really has (their "lakehouse" and the
  posting's "lakehouse platform"; "Airflow" when the posting says "orchestration")
- reorder skill groups and items so posting keywords come first; drop irrelevant skills
- compress old or irrelevant roles into an "Earlier Experience" line
- surface an adjacent skill honestly: "streaming with Kinesis" can answer a request for
  "Kafka or similar". Never write "Kafka".

These stay fixed: employer names, official titles, dates, degrees, and anything the user
said "no" to.

## Generic CV (no posting)

Aim at the roles the user said they want. If they skipped that question, aim at the
natural next step from their current role. Balance breadth and depth, and keep the
strongest achievements from every recent role.

## Style

- US English and US spelling (organize, optimize, modeling, program, center).
- Consistent tense, date format and capitalization of tool names (PostgreSQL, dbt,
  GitHub, AWS).
- Spell out an acronym once if the target reader might not know it, unless it's a
  standard in the field.
- Contact details in the body, a single column, no tables, text boxes or images for
  content. The renderer already does this.

## Trimming to two pages

When the render is still over 2 pages after autofit, cut in this order, re-rendering
between steps:

1. bullets of roles older than about 10 years or irrelevant to the target
2. old roles collapsed into one "Earlier Experience" line each
3. the weakest bullets in mid-career roles (keep at least 2 per role)
4. the skills list: remove items irrelevant to the target, merge groups
5. education details, certifications and courses that don't serve the target
6. word-level tightening of long bullets and the summary

Never cut the header and contact details, the summary, the current role's key bullets,
or the degree itself.
